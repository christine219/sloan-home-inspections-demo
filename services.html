Property types

Which inspection fits your plans?

Residential Homes

A residential home inspection evaluates the home’s visible, accessible systems and components.

Exterior: Roof, siding, foundation, drainage, windows, doors, decks, and porches.

Interior: Plumbing, electrical, heating and cooling, appliances, walls, floors, ceilings, attics, and crawlspaces.

We help you understand the home’s condition and identify potential concerns before you buy.

Townhomes

A townhome inspection evaluates the home’s visible, accessible systems and components, including plumbing, electrical, heating and cooling, appliances, and interior features.

Accessible exterior components, attics, and crawlspaces are evaluated as applicable. Inspection scope may vary based on the property and HOA responsibilities.

We help you understand the home’s condition and identify potential concerns before you buy.

Condos & Condotels

A condo or condotel inspection evaluates the unit’s visible, accessible components, including plumbing, electrical, heating and cooling, appliances, windows, doors, walls, ceilings, and floors.

The scope generally focuses on the individual unit. Shared systems, common areas, and building exteriors may fall under the association’s responsibility and outside the inspection scope.

We help you understand the unit’s condition and identify potential concerns before you buy.

Manufactured Homes

A manufactured home inspection evaluates the home’s visible, accessible condition so a buyer understands safety concerns, repair needs, and maintenance issues before purchasing.

It covers the same major systems as a traditional home inspection—roof, exterior, plumbing, electrical, heating and cooling, interior, and structural components—with extra attention to how the home is supported and what’s underneath it. The inspection is visual; it does not uncover every concealed defect.

Pre-Listing Inspections

A pre-listing inspection is a home inspection the seller schedules before putting the property on the market. It helps the seller understand the home’s condition and identify concerns before a buyer’s inspection.

The inspector evaluates visible, accessible components—including the roof, structure, plumbing, electrical, heating and cooling—and provides a report explaining the findings.

Your home inspection

Scope of Your Home Inspection

A home inspection evaluates the condition of accessible systems and components at the time of the inspection. Your property type, access, and agreed scope determine what can be inspected.

Roof & Exterior

Accessible roof components, exterior surfaces, and visible drainage or condition concerns.

Structure & Accessible Spaces

Visible structural components and accessible attic or crawlspace areas, where present and safe to enter.

Plumbing

Accessible plumbing components, fixtures, and visible leaks or operational concerns.

Electrical

Accessible electrical components and visible safety or condition concerns within the inspection scope.

Heating & Cooling

Accessible equipment and operation where conditions allow safe testing.

Interior Components

Accessible interior surfaces, doors, windows, and installed components within the agreed scope.

Additional services have their own scope. A standard home inspection does not include every test or specialist evaluation. Sewer camera scopes, laboratory sampling, septic services, and engineer-issued foundation certifications are selected separately.

Additional services

Enhance your inspection.

Choose services based on your property, concerns, and lender's checklist. Our team can help you plan the scope.

Air Quality & Mold

An indoor air quality inspection evaluates conditions that may affect the air inside a home, such as moisture, ventilation problems, and selected airborne contaminants. The exact scope matters—there is no single test that checks for every air quality concern.

Sewer Lateral / Camera Scope

A sewer scope inspection uses a specialized camera to examine the inside of the main wastewater line running from the home toward the public sewer connection or septic tank. It helps identify visible problems underground that a standard home inspection cannot reveal. See the sewer scope guide.

Water Testing

Well water testing checks a sample of a home’s private well water for specific bacteria, chemicals, or water-quality indicators. It helps buyers identify concerns that may not be noticeable by taste, smell, or appearance. Samples should be analyzed by a state-certified drinking water laboratory.

Lead Testing

A lead-based paint inspection determines whether a home contains lead-based paint and identifies where it is located. It is especially useful for homes built before 1978, before buying or renovating. A certified lead inspector or risk assessor tests painted surfaces with an X-ray fluorescence (XRF) analyzer or laboratory paint-chip samples. The written report identifies tested surfaces and results. This specialized service requires qualifications separate from a standard home inspection.

Pool Inspections

A pool inspection evaluates the pool’s visible condition and accessible equipment to help buyers understand repair needs and safety concerns before purchasing a home. It includes visual checks and operation of equipment using normal controls when conditions allow.

Irrigation Inspections

An irrigation inspection checks the visible condition and operation of a home’s lawn sprinkler system. It can identify damaged components, watering problems, and signs of leaks that may waste water or require repairs. It is available as an additional service with a home inspection.

Manufactured Home Foundation Certification

A manufactured home foundation certification, often called an engineer’s certification, documents whether the foundation meets applicable permanent-foundation requirements. A lender may request it for a purchase or refinance. Learn why certification may be needed.

Septic Inspection Coordination

A septic inspection evaluates the condition and operation of a home’s private wastewater system. It can help buyers identify signs of damage, backups, or system failure before purchasing. A typical system includes a tank and a drainfield, where wastewater disperses into the soil for further treatment. We coordinate this separate service through Coastal Septic.

Reinspections

A reinspection is a follow-up visit to check specific items identified in the original inspection report, usually after the seller or a contractor reports that repairs are complete.

Before you schedule

Good questions. Clear answers.

How do I choose the right additional services?

Start with the property’s systems and your concerns: a private well, a sewer connection, a pool, or indoor air concerns may call for a separate service. If you are using VA, FHA, or USDA financing, our team can help coordinate services while your lender confirms its requirements.

Is a condo or townhome inspection the same as a house inspection?

The scope depends on the property and accessible components serving the unit. Common areas and shared systems may fall under the association’s responsibility. Confirm the scope with our team and review association documents with your agent.

Does a manufactured home inspection include HUD foundation certification?

No. A home inspection evaluates accessible home systems and components. A foundation certification is a separate engineering service your lender may request. Learn about manufactured home certifications.

What should I have ready when scheduling?

Have the address, property type, approximate size, desired inspection date, and any closing or builder deadlines ready. Coordinate access and utilities with the seller, builder, or property manager; our team will help confirm preparation details.

Does a home inspection replace the lender’s appraisal?

No. An appraisal estimates value for the lender; a home inspection evaluates accessible systems and components to help you understand the home’s condition. Explore inspection services for VA, FHA, and USDA financing.
