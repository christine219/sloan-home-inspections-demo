New Construction

Inspections at Every Construction Stage

Building a new home involves decisions at every stage. Independent inspections can document visible concerns before the slab is poured, before drywall covers the framing and systems, and before closing. An 11-month inspection gives you a chance to review the home again before your builder’s one-year warranty deadline.

01 /

Pre-Slab

Pre-slab inspections evaluate accessible plumbing within the footings and slab, slab grading, and the vapor barrier (encapsulation) before concrete is poured.

02 /

Pre-Drywall

Pre-drywall inspections evaluate visible framing and the home’s plumbing, electrical, and HVAC rough-ins before walls are closed. This stage allows accessible installation details and potential concerns to be identified while these systems remain visible.

03 /

Final Inspection

With construction complete, the final inspection evaluates the home’s operation, function, and visible condition—from roof to foundation and the accessible systems and components in between. Findings help you discuss concerns with your builder before closing.

04 /

11-Month Warranty Inspection

An 11-month warranty inspection is scheduled before your builder’s one-year warranty expires.

Plan before your 11-month warranty deadline

  • Checks visible, accessible components, including the roof, plumbing, electrical, HVAC, attic, and crawlspace where accessible.
  • Identifies concerns that have appeared since move-in.
  • Documents findings with photos and explanations for your builder.
  • Helps you submit repair requests before the warranty deadline.

Your builder’s warranty determines coverage. Schedule early enough to review the report and submit claims on time.

Additional Inspection Services

Evaluate systems and concerns beyond the standard home inspection with separately selected services, including sewer scopes, air quality assessments, and water testing.

Sewer Camera Scopes

Sewer scoping is a camera inspection of the home’s main sewer line. We run a specialized camera through an accessible cleanout to look inside the pipe that carries wastewater from the home toward the public sewer connection or septic tank.

Explore sewer scopes

Air Quality & Mold

An indoor air quality inspection evaluates conditions that may affect the air inside a home, such as moisture, ventilation problems, and selected airborne contaminants. The exact scope matters—there is no single test that checks for every air quality concern.

Explore air quality services

Water Testing

Well water testing checks a sample of a home’s private well water for specific bacteria, chemicals, or water-quality indicators. It helps buyers identify concerns that may not be noticeable by taste, smell, or appearance. Samples should be analyzed by a state-certified drinking water laboratory.

Six questions about home inspections.

Buying, building, or planning your next step? Start with these answers—and call our team when you want to talk through your property.

What does a home inspection cover—and what does it leave out?

A home inspection evaluates accessible systems and components at the time of the inspection, such as the roof, structure, plumbing, electrical, heating and cooling, and interior components within the agreed scope. It helps identify visible concerns and maintenance needs. It cannot uncover every concealed condition or predict future failures. Laboratory testing, sewer camera scopes, septic inspections, and engineer-issued foundation certifications are separate services. Explore what’s included.

Do I need an independent inspection if my new home has a Certificate of Occupancy?

Yes—these serve different purposes. Local building inspections focus on applicable code requirements and occupancy approval. An independent home inspection gives you a separate assessment of accessible systems and components, with findings you can discuss with your builder. A Certificate of Occupancy does not mean a home is defect-free. Understand the CO and home inspection difference.

When should I schedule my new construction inspections?

Contact us early so inspections fit your builder’s schedule. A pre-slab inspection occurs before concrete is poured; a pre-drywall inspection occurs before walls conceal framing and systems. A final inspection reviews the completed home before closing. An 11-month inspection helps document concerns before your builder’s one-year warranty ends—confirm your actual warranty terms and deadlines. Explore the four construction checkpoints.

Which additional services should I consider for my property?

Choose based on the home’s systems, age, your concerns, and any lender requirements. A sewer scope looks inside accessible sewer piping; well water testing checks the quality of an independent water supply for the selected test panel. Air quality or mold assessment, lead testing, pool and irrigation inspections, and septic coordination have separate scopes. New homes can also benefit from relevant additional testing. Our team can help you understand the options. Compare additional services.

How do I prepare for the inspection and use the report afterward?

Coordinate property access and utility availability with the seller, builder, or property manager before the appointment. Let our team know about specific concerns, deadlines, and any access restrictions. Afterward, review the report’s photos, observations, and recommendations. Ask us for clarification, then discuss next steps with your agent or builder and appropriate specialists. An inspection documents conditions; it does not pass or fail the home or decide which repairs a seller must make. Learn about your inspection report.

Is a home inspection the same as an appraisal for VA, FHA, or USDA financing?

No. An appraisal estimates value for the lender and may identify property conditions that need attention. A home inspection provides a closer look at accessible systems and components for your understanding. Depending on the property and lender, separate services may include well water testing, septic inspection, or an engineer’s manufactured home foundation certification. These are not automatically required for every loan. Unsure what you need? Our experienced team can help coordinate inspection services while your lender confirms the requirements.
