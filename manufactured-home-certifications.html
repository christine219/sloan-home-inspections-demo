Manufactured Home Foundation Certifications

Ensure Your Manufactured Home Meets HUD Compliance Requirements.

The exact review depends on the home, site, lender request, and engineer’s scope.

Buying or refinancing a manufactured home? Your lender may request an engineer’s certification showing whether the permanent foundation meets the applicable requirements.

Why it's necessaryWhat is reviewedHow to prepare

Why lenders ask
for a certification.

A manufactured home is built in a factory and then installed on a site. The lender may need written confirmation about the foundation supporting and anchoring the home.

For FHA transactions where foundation certification is required, the foundation is evaluated against HUD's Permanent Foundations Guide for Manufactured Housing. Other loan programs and lenders may request their own documentation. Confirm the exact requirement before you order.

Skirting alone does not establish compliance.

What you see around the outside does not tell the whole story. The foundation review addresses the supporting system and attachment to the site, using the documentation and conditions available for evaluation.

Who issues the certification?

A licensed professional engineer evaluates the applicable foundation information and issues the engineer’s certification when supported by the findings. Sloan helps arrange the on-site inspection and certification process. A home inspection report by itself is not an engineer’s certification.

Beyond a general home inspection

What the review may address.

Support & Foundation

Visible piers, footings, supporting components, and how the home is installed on its site.

Anchoring & Connections

Accessible anchorage and attachment details relevant to the applicable permanent foundation criteria.

Site & Alterations

Relevant site conditions and additions or modifications that may affect the engineering evaluation. A deck, porch, or attached structure may require additional review.

Certification and home inspection answer different questions. The engineer’s certification addresses the specified foundation criteria. A residential home inspection looks at accessible home systems and components. Buyers may need both.

HUD identification labels, data plates, title status, and other loan documents are separate items. A foundation certification does not replace missing identification documents or guarantee loan eligibility.

Start early.
Keep closing moving.

  1. Get the lender's request. Send the loan program, property address, closing deadline, and the exact certification or report requested.
  2. Arrange access. Provide safe access beneath the home and to relevant exterior areas. Share available prior certifications and information about additions or foundation changes.
  3. Complete the site inspection. The required observations, photographs, and measurements are gathered for engineering review.
  4. Receive the review outcome. If concerns prevent certification, repairs, additional information, or a follow-up review may be needed before a certification can be issued.

Buying, selling, or refinancing?

Order the requested certification early enough to allow time for review and any follow-up. Send the result to your lender, who determines whether it satisfies the loan file.

Can an older certification be reused?

Ask your lender. Acceptance depends on the transaction, program requirements, the previous report, and whether the home or foundation has changed. Do not assume an old report will be accepted.

Does the certification guarantee financing?

No. It supplies specific foundation documentation. The lender still evaluates the other property and borrower requirements.
